Why does Swiss cheese have fewer holes now? Because milking got too clean. And Swiss cheesemakers had to take their own government to court to get the holes back.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

The holes are gas, from bacteria in the cheese. But a bubble needs a place to start.

Swiss scientists found it. Specks of hay. Hay is full of tiny tubes, so a speck can carry a little air. The gas gathers there, and a hole grows.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Those specks used to fall into open milk buckets. But from about 2005, Swiss farms started switching to closed milking machines. Fewer specks, fewer holes.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Emmentaler, the original Swiss cheese, has an official rulebook. So its makers asked to add a pinch of ground hay to the milk. Switzerland's agriculture office said no, because it wasn't traditional.

In 2025, a Swiss court sided with the cheesemakers. The court said the hay powder isn't traditional, but the holes it brings back are.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

In America, cheesemakers wanted smaller holes. Cheese with big holes can get torn up in slicing machines. In 2001, the USDA changed its rules. Good Swiss cheese used to need holes about the size of a dime. Now they can be as small as a pea.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

So today, Switzerland's smallest holes are about the size of America's biggest.

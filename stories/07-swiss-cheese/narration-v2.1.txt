Why does Swiss cheese have fewer holes now? Because milking got too clean. And Swiss cheesemakers had to take their own government to court to get the holes back.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

The holes are gas, from bacteria in the cheese. But a bubble needs a place to start.

Swiss scientists found it. Specks of hay. Hay is full of tiny tubes, so each speck carries a little air. The gas gathers there, and a hole grows.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Those specks used to fall into open milk buckets. But from about 2005, Swiss farms switched to closed milking machines. Fewer specks, fewer holes.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

So the makers of Emmentaler, the original Swiss cheese, asked to add a pinch of ground hay to the milk. Switzerland's agriculture office said no, because it wasn't traditional.

In 2025, a Swiss court sided with the cheesemakers. The court said the hay powder isn't traditional, but the holes it brings back are.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

In America, the holes got smaller on purpose. Back in 2001, the smallest hole the USDA's top grade asks for went from about a dime to about a pea. Cheese with big holes gets torn up in slicing machines.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

So on paper, the smallest hole Switzerland asks for is about the biggest hole America's top grade asks for.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

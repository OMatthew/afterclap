Where did haunted houses come from?
The usual story: in the 1930s, Halloween pranks got out of hand, so grown-ups made haunted houses to keep kids busy instead.
But old newspapers say the idea is older.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In 1915, a church youth class in South Dakota turned their minister's house into "A Haunted House."
Guests were sent, two at a time, up "the stairway that leads to death." At the top, a ghost pounced.
Another party plan had guests touch a pretend dead man's "brains" in the dark: an ice-cold sponge. His "eyes"? Two peeled grapes.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
So why does everyone point to the 1930s? Because the pranks were real. Kids tipped over outhouses and covered windows in soap.
Some towns threw big Halloween parties to keep them busy. In one Wisconsin town, police said the damage dropped a lot.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Then people saw a haunted house could raise money.
In the 1960s, a volunteer women's group in Indianapolis sold tickets to one in an old building, and raised thousands.
Then clubs around the country started running their own, for charity.
[[PAUSE 0.5]]
So decades before that Indianapolis fundraiser, a church party was sending guests upstairs to meet a ghost.

Where did haunted houses come from?
The usual story puts them in the Depression, when towns wanted to keep kids from wrecking things on Halloween.
But old newspapers say the idea is older.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In 1915, a church youth class in South Dakota turned their minister's house into "A Haunted House."
Guests were sent, two at a time, up "the stairway that leads to death." At the top, a ghost pounced.
Another party plan had guests touch a dead man's "brains" in the dark: an ice-cold sponge. His "eyes"? Two peeled grapes.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Later, in the Depression, pranks got rough, so some towns threw big parties instead.
In Fargo, the paper's headline afterward: "Halloween Damage Light."
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Then people found another use for a haunted house: raising money.
Decades later, a volunteer women's group in Indianapolis sold tickets to one in an old building, and raised thousands.
Then clubs around the country started running their own, for charity.
[[PAUSE 0.5]]
So decades before that Indianapolis fundraiser, a church party was sending guests upstairs to meet a ghost.

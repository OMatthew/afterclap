# lf01-minefields: script review (independent, Fable, 2026-11-13)

Read: CLAUDE.md, CHANNEL_RULES.md, research/longform/ (research file, topic pick, genre survey), narration.txt, STORYBOARD.md. Facts checked today against the pages listed at the bottom. Nothing else in the repo was changed.

## VERDICT: FIX FIRST

The spine is good and most facts hold. Four lines are wrong or overstated, the title's "why" is never answered in the countries' own words, the last line is false as written, and the read will probably land under 8:00. All fixes below are small. With them it is ready to voice.

---

## BLOCKING fixes (with replacement text)

### B1. Ypres 2014: "as they dug" is wrong
The court account (Brussels Times, 2024 ruling) says the shells were dug up, moved behind the building and left for weeks before one exploded. Reuters at the time: four workers "behind a warehouse under construction", circumstances under investigation.
- Now: "In 2014, two construction workers died in Ypres when a First World War shell went off as they dug."
- Use: **"In 2014, two construction workers were killed in Ypres when a First World War shell exploded on a building site."**
- Storyboard beat 19 (digger and trench) needs to change with it.

### B2. The ending is false: the five charges are not "under a farm"
Simon Jones: three are under the Birdcage field, one is about 100 yards from the rebuilt La Petite Douve farm, and one (Peckham, 20,000 lb) is "exactly" under the rebuilt farm. That last detail is the best fact in the story and the script never uses it.
- Now: "Under a farm near Messines, five charges are still waiting, more than a century after they were laid. / And along Europe's eastern edge, countries are getting ready to bury more."
- Use (plain version):
  **"What we do know is this. Near Messines, five charges are still in the ground, more than a century after they were laid. One of them is under a farmhouse.
  And along Europe's eastern edge, new mines are on order. For now, they're meant for storage."**
- This also fixes two other problems: the old last line restated the hook (pre-send check 5), and "getting ready to bury more" says more than "stored, not laid".
- Optional last beat, if the lead thinks it's earned (it is true: Jones says the Birdcage charges were laid in spring 1916 for an attack planned for that June): "The ones in Belgium were only meant to wait a few weeks." I would voice both and pick by ear; it is close to a mic-drop.
- Plant the farmhouse earlier too (see B3).

### B3. The six lost charges: one cause is wrong, and add the farmhouse
Jones: at La Petite Douve the Germans broke into a side chamber; it was a German charge two days later that smashed 400 feet of the main gallery and cut off the mine.
- Now: "Some sat outside the attack. One was cut off when German miners broke into its tunnel. One was lost when its tunnel flooded."
- Use: **"Four sat at the far end of the line, outside the attack. One was cut off when a German blast wrecked its tunnel. One was lost when its tunnel flooded."**
- Then, after "Nobody can say what state they're in.", add: **"After the war, one farm was rebuilt right on top of one of them."**

### B4. RUSI's 500 m is what Russia aimed for, and the manuals are Russia's
RUSI (Stormbreak): "from a doctrinal assumption of 120 metres to a practical aim to make them 500 metres deep."
- Now: "In places, its minefields ran about five hundred meters deep, about four times what military manuals call for."
- Use: **"Russia set out to make its minefields about five hundred meters deep. That's about four times what its own manuals called for."**

### B5. Finland: "made at home, starting in 2028" is a hope, not a plan
AFP/STT via Al Jazeera, 19 Oct 2026: Finland "is seeking to acquire one million"; it "would like" domestic makers, contracts by end of 2027, "production up and running by 2028"; buying abroad "is not totally ruled out".
- Now: "Finland plans to buy about a million anti-personnel mines, made at home, starting in 2028."
- Use: **"Finland wants about a million anti-personnel mines. It hopes to have them made at home, with production starting by 2028."**

### B6. The video never says why they left (the title's question), or who objected
The only reason given is "countries on Russia's border". The viewer has to infer the answer to the title, and the Diana section before it leans one way with no reply. One line each fixes both. Sources: all five governments cited the Russian threat (research file, section 7); the UN secretary-general said he was "gravely concerned" and the Red Cross president warned of "a dangerous shift" (Landmine Monitor 2025).
- After "They were the first countries ever to leave it.", add:
  **"All five gave the same reason: the threat from Russia. The Red Cross and the head of the United Nations warned that civilians would pay for it, for decades."**
- Then: "That same year, Lebanon joined." (unchanged)

### B7. The expert section states one view in the narrator's own voice
"Politics, terrain and how armies are led matter more." has no "they say", so the narrator takes a side. "Attackers always adapt" is stronger than Kofman or RUSI put it, and "they already are" is a present-tense claim we can't freeze on upload day.
- Use:
  **"Some experts think so. Drones that watch everything make it very hard to gather an army for an attack without being seen and hit.
  Others say armies adapt. They point to parts of the front in Ukraine where new ways of attacking have started to work.
  And some scholars doubt that weapons decide this at all. They say politics, terrain and how armies are led matter more.
  Nobody knows yet which of them is right."**
- With that, the three views are fair to Watling/RUSI, Kofman/RUSI (Oct 2025 paper), and Lieber.

### B8. Small wording fixes (cheap, each avoids a correction in the comments)
- **"By nightfall, most of the ridge was in British hands."** The whole ridge fell that day (Anzac Memorial: "By the end of the day, one of the strongest positions on the Western Front had fallen"). Use: **"By the end of the day, the ridge was in British hands."**
- **"from the English Channel to Switzerland."** The line met the sea at Nieuwpoort, on the North Sea. Use: **"from the North Sea to Switzerland."** (Storyboard beats 8 and 12 list too.)
- **"the treaty Diana helped inspire."** The treaty talks began in October 1996, before her walk. Use: **"the treaty signed after Diana's walk."**
- **"drones have taken over a job that used to need a whole team."** Overstated and vague (spotting is not clearing). Use: **"Since then, drones have changed the job on both sides. They spot mines from the air. And they drop new ones."**
- **"a problem nobody had planned for."** Use **"few had planned for."**

### B9. Runtime: this will likely come in under 8:00
1,147 words at 150 wpm is 7:39 before pauses. The storyboard clock runs to about 9:40, which assumes about 120 wpm; one of the two is wrong. The fixes above add about 70 words, and the two retention lines in O1 and O3 add about 45, giving roughly 1,260 words, or 8:20-8:30 with pauses. Measure the first voice render before drawing; if it is under 8:10, add substance from O4, not pauses.

## Retention and structure

**Cold open (0:00-0:35): strong.** "And the field blew up", the cow, "it wasn't the only one" all work. The weak line is the promise: "To see why, start with why that one was under a Belgian field" is hard to hear once (why... why), and the Belgian mine does not explain the 2025 decision, so it promises a link the video doesn't deliver.

**Where I would drift:**
1. **0:55-1:40, the 1914 primer.** This audience knows machine guns and barbed wire. It is the first thing after the hook and it is the most familiar minute in the video.
2. **About 3:55-5:50 (iron harvest, zone rouge, mines that wait, Diana).** Two minutes with the mystery closed and the Europe payoff not yet in sight. There is no line telling me the video is still heading to the present. Diana and Harry are the softest stretch for young men.
3. **The Europe list (five countries, Lebanon, the US, Finland, Poland, Estonia).** Six facts in a row joined by "and then".

**Turns:** there is one roughly every 60-90 s through Messines. After "five charges remain" the next real turn is the invasion of Ukraine, about two minutes later.

**Is the Ukraine/Europe half earned?** Mostly. The hinge "fired by men, at a time they chose / a landmine waits" is the best line in the script and makes the second half belong. What's missing is the reason (B6) and a signpost in the middle.

**Ending:** the open question is the right place to stop. The last two lines need B2.

## OPTIONAL improvements (in order of value)

### O1. Rewrite the promise line
**"Seventy years later, countries along Europe's eastern border are getting ready to put explosives in the ground again.
This is how we got from that field to that border."**

### O2. Tighten the 1914 primer and give it a turn
**"In the autumn of 1914, the armies of the First World War ran into a problem few had planned for. Defending had become far easier than attacking.
A machine gun could fire hundreds of bullets a minute. Barbed wire held attackers in the open. And while an attack crawled forward, the defender could bring up fresh troops by train.
So both sides dug in."**
(Drops the artillery line and saves a drawing in beat 7.)

### O3. Mid-video signpost, after the zone rouge
**"Europe has spent a hundred years digging that war out of the ground.
In 2025, five countries decided they needed to be able to put something back."**
Then the hinge ("The Messines mines were fired by men..."). This is the re-hook the middle lacks, and it's true as worded (ability, not laying).

### O4. Trade Harry for Ukraine
Cut "In 2025, her son, Prince Harry, went back to Angola to see the same work still going on." It restates Cambodia's point. If the read needs words (B9), spend them on Ukraine, where this audience is most interested: one concrete picture of what a minefield did to an attack in June 2023 (RUSI's Stormbreak has sourced detail; verify before use). Don't add anything unsourced.

### O5. Break up the Europe list with "but"
After the Finland, Poland and Estonia lines: **"But none of it is in the ground. For now, they say, the mines will be stored. They'd be laid only if an attack seemed close."**

### O6. Numbers
The Ukraine paragraph has five figures in 35 seconds (500 m, four times, 130,000, Greece, 28 billion). Drop the square kilometers by ear and keep Greece: "Ukraine says an area about the size of Greece may be contaminated." The exact figure can be written on screen.

## Neutrality and safety

- With B6 and B7 the script takes no side and predicts nothing. Without them it leans slightly (unanswered humanitarian framing, then one expert view in the narrator's voice).
- "So, is defense winning again, the way it did in 1915?" is fine as a question.
- "Russia launched its full-scale invasion" is the standard factual wording. Keep.
- Casualties are handled calmly. "Soldier or child, it can't tell the difference" is fair and matches the treaty's own language. The 10,000 German dead figure is rightly left out.
- "The ground above was a killing field" is the one phrase with heat. "The ground above couldn't be crossed" is calmer and just as clear. Optional.
- Don't draw the Ypres workers or any blast there (already planned).

---

## Title and thumbnail

- **"Why Europe is bringing back landmines"** is acceptable. "Bringing back" is true of arsenals (treaty exits, orders, factories) and doesn't claim they're laid. "Planting", "burying" or "mining its borders" would overclaim. It doesn't give the answer away. It's the right primary title.
- The alt ("Lightning set off a bomb from 1917. Five more are still buried.") sells a different, smaller video than the one we made, and has two numbers.
- Three more to test:
  1. **Why Russia's neighbors want their landmines back** (more specific than "Europe", and names the tension)
  2. **Europe banned landmines. Now it's buying them again.**
  3. **The explosives Europe buried, and the ones it's ordering now** (ties both halves; weaker hook)
- **Thumbnail:** the farmhouse cut-away with one red charge is true (Peckham) and pairs well with the Europe title, because it adds a second puzzle. Use "STILL THERE" rather than no text; with no text the link to the title is too far. Don't draw anything that looks like mines along a border.

## Storyboard notes

**Fix for accuracy**
- **Beats 4, 15, 16, 37:** five red marks under one field or one farmhouse is wrong. Under the 1955 field show the three neighbours beside the crater (true, and more unsettling), then widen to all five. The farmhouse gets one mark.
- **Beats 5 and 37:** a red line along the eastern border reads as "mines are laid there". Use red crates or dots set back from a plain ink border, or a dashed line that never fills.
- **Beat 31:** the red fill reads as confirmed minefield; the figure is suspected area. Use loose hatching, and slide Greece's outline over it instead of side by side.
- **Beat 19:** see B1. Reuse the shell pile from beat 18 by a wall; no digger.

**Highest-value ideas**
1. **One ground line for the whole film.** Keep the same soil cross-section as the strip's backbone: the 1955 crater, the 1917 tunnels at 20 m, shells in the plough layer, a mine just under the grass, the Ukrainian belt. Red is always below the line, until the last minute, when it sits above ground in crates. The viewer feels the ending without being told.
2. **The 500 m ruler (beat 28).** Draw a bracket marked "120 m" first, then stretch it (nudge) to "500 m" as the line is spoken, with mines filling in behind it.
3. **Nineteen charges as a countdown (beats 12-13).** As the camera travels the ridge, each charge fills red; at 3:10 they go in a ripple left to right over a second or two, and six stay lit. That one shot sets up the next chapter with no words.
4. **Return to the 1955 frame at "the lightning found one of them"** (beat 15): same drawing as beat 2, now with the tunnel and three neighbours visible below. It pays off the cold open and costs nothing.
5. **Five countries (beat 32):** fill each on its exit date, written small beside it, then one small mark for Lebanon appearing far to the south.

**Too costly, or cut**
- 37 beats is about 35 drawings; the topic pick budgeted 25-30. Merge 6+7 (O2 removes a vignette), 17+18, 24+25 (same lane, deminer then Diana), 33+34.
- Beat 35's three panels: reuse the drone (30) and the stopped vehicle (29); only the book is new.
- Sixteen hand-written numbers is too many to land. Keep about eight: 1955, 3:10, 19, 5, 70 t, 122, 500 m, 600.
- Beat 22's world map plus two Europe maps plus Ukraine plus France: draw one Europe base map and reuse it for beats 5, 8, 20, 27, 32.

Why are oranges orange? Cool nights. Where nights stay warm, a ripe orange can stay green. And green oranges ended up at the Supreme Court.

Cool nights break down the peel's green, and orange takes over.

In Florida, early oranges ripen green. From 1921, packers gassed them to clear the green, but they came out pale yellow.

So in the 1930s, Florida began dyeing some of them orange, marked "Color Added."

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In the 1950s, the government re-tested its food dyes, and the orange dye failed. In 1955, it was struck off the list. Florida's growers took it to court.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1958, the Supreme Court ruled, unanimously: under the law, a dye that isn't harmless can't be used at all. Not even a little.

So Congress allowed a new orange dye, until it could write a new color law with safe limits. That law came in 1960, partly because of oranges. It's the law now taking Red No. 3, the cherry dye, out of our food.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

And that new orange dye? It was never banned. The last batch was certified in 2020. Now the FDA wants it off the books, as abandoned.

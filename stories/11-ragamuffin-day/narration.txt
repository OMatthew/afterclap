Why did New York kids once go door to door in costume on Thanksgiving? Because they were copying the grown-ups.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Around 1870, men's clubs in New York paraded in costume on Thanksgiving. Kids copied them, in old clothes many sizes too large.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

The grown-ups gave it up. The kids didn't. They went door to door asking, "Anything for Thanksgiving?" Pennies, apples, candy.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Adults fought back. A newspaper called the doorbell ringing "long past a joke." Police were told to stop the begging.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Then a boys' club marched kids in costume behind a slogan: "American boys do not beg." By about 1940, the begging was gone.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Right then, a new custom reached New York from out West. Kids in costume, ringing doorbells for treats. On Halloween. And some adults called it begging, all over again.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

In Brooklyn, kids still parade in their parents' too-big clothes every year. Just in October now.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

And why does the Macy's parade have giant balloons? That's another story.

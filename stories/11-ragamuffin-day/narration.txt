Why did New York kids once go door to door in costume on Thanksgiving? Because they were copying the grown-ups. Then the city spent decades trying to stop them.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Around 1870, a Brooklyn pastor said, men's clubs paraded in costume on Thanksgiving. Kids copied them, in old clothes many sizes too large.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

The men gave up their parades. The kids didn't. They rang doorbells and asked, "Anything for Thanksgiving?" Pennies, apples, candy.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

A newspaper said ringing every doorbell for handouts was "long past a joke." Police were told to stop the begging. A boys' club held its own costume parade, with the slogan "American boys do not beg." By about 1940, the begging was gone.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Within a few years, a new custom reached New York from out West. Kids in costume, ringing doorbells for treats. On Halloween. And some adults called it begging, all over again.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

In the 1960s, Brooklyn brought the costumes back as the Ragamuffin Parade. Kids still march every fall. Just not on Thanksgiving.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

And why does the Macy's parade have giant balloons? That's another story.

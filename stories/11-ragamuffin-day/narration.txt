Why did New York kids once go door to door in costume on Thanksgiving? Because they were copying the grown-ups. Then the city spent decades trying to stop them.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Back in the 1800s, grown men's clubs paraded through New York in costume on Thanksgiving. So kids copied them, in old clothes many sizes too large.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Then the men gave up their parades. The kids didn't. They rang doorbells and asked, "Anything for Thanksgiving?" Pennies, apples, candy.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

So the grown-ups pushed back. A newspaper said ringing every doorbell for handouts was "long past a joke." Police were told to stop the begging. A boys' club held its own costume parade, with the slogan "American boys do not beg." By about 1940, the begging was gone.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

But the doorbells didn't stay quiet for long. Within a decade, a new custom reached New York from out West. Kids in costume, ringing doorbells for treats. Only now, on Halloween.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

And some grown-ups called it begging, all over again.

Why did New York kids once go door to door in costume on Thanksgiving? Because they were copying the grown-ups. Then other grown-ups spent decades trying to stop the kids.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Back in the 1800s, grown men's clubs paraded through New York in costume on Thanksgiving. So kids copied them, in old clothes many sizes too large. They looked like ragamuffins, scruffy kids in rags. So people called the day Ragamuffin Day.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

But the men gave up their parades. The kids didn't. They rang doorbells and asked, "Anything for Thanksgiving?" Pennies, apples, candy.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

So the grown-ups pushed back. A newspaper called the kids a nuisance. Police were told to stop the begging. A boys' club held a costume parade instead, with the slogan "American boys do not beg." By about 1940, Ragamuffin Day had faded.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

But the doorbells didn't stay quiet for long. Within ten years, trick-or-treating spread from the West to the East Coast. Kids in costume, ringing doorbells for treats. Only now, on Halloween.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

And some adults on the East Coast called it begging, all over again.

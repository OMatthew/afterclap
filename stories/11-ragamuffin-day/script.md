## Review log
- v1 (lead): research chain beats 1–6; tease for 12 "Why does the Macy's parade have giant balloons?" (research primary).
- Fact check (Fable): attribute 1870 to the Brooklyn pastor (one man's memory, 1909); "Right then" → "Within a few years" (trick-or-treating reached the East in the late 1940s, Kawash); the Bay Ridge parade is now held in late September and kids wear ordinary costumes, so not "October" and not "still in their parents' clothes"; start year disputed (1966/1967) → "the 1960s". Tease checked (1924 zoo animals scared children; balloons from 1927; released with rewards; 1932 plane). Link to Macy's is "overshadowed", never "descended".
- Listener panel (Opus): hook answers in 3 s and promises nothing → add the conflict to the hook; "Adults fought back" is stock; make the boys' club parade clearly a rival parade; name the Ragamuffin Parade once; "Just in October now" is a clever shortcut and suggests an unbroken custom.
- v2 (lead): merged both; the Brooklyn parade is framed as a revival ("brought the costumes back"), not a survival. Hook conflict line "Then the city spent decades trying to stop them" (1903 editorial to about 1940; "the city" = papers, police, schools).
- v2.1 (lead, 2026-10-19, story-engine check from Kallaway): stakes are in the hook ("the city spent decades trying to stop them"), the mid re-hook is "The men gave up their parades. The kids didn't." The Halloween beat joined only by "and then", so it now turns on a "but": "But the doorbells didn't stay quiet for long." 185 words.
- v2.2 (Matthew, 2026-10-20): "a Brooklyn pastor said" was a hedge (one man's 1909 memory of "about 1870") with no payoff: now "Back in the 1800s, grown men's clubs paraded through New York in costume on Thanksgiving" (documented: Fantasticals, Sun 1907, Herald 1922), no pastor, no year. Ending "Kids still march every fall. Just not on Thanksgiving." was indirect and didn't land: now a plain call-back to the opening image, "And every fall, Brooklyn kids still march in a costume parade, in old clothes many sizes too large." (Bay Ridge Ragamuffin Parade, since 1966, parents' oversized clothing.)
- v3 draft (lead, 2026-10-20, after Matthew asked what the Ragamuffin Parade is): beats turn on so/but; no pastor; ends on the Halloween twist ("And some grown-ups called it begging, all over again."), the Brooklyn parade moves to the description. 176 words. Under review: blind A/B (v2.1 vs v3) listener panel and a v3 fact-check (Sol).
- Reviews (Sol, 2026-10-20; ~/Movies/Afterclap/review/): blind A/B panel (11-compare-sol.md) picked v3 over v2.1 (all three listeners; A's pastor line and parade epilogue were the weak spots). Rule added: name the thing, with context; don't cut it.

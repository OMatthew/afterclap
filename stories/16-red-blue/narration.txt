Why are Republican states red, and Democratic states blue?
Once, it depended on which channel you watched.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In 1976, NBC built a giant lit-up map. Republicans were blue and Democrats were red, copied from Britain, where the conservatives are blue.
Other networks picked their own colors. Flip channels on election night, and the same state could change color.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Gradually, the networks settled on red for Republicans and blue for Democrats.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Then came the 2000 election, Bush against Gore. The result stayed undecided for five weeks of recounts and court cases.
Night after night, the same red-and-blue map stayed on TV.
That year, the New York Times ran a color election map too. One of its graphics editors picked red for Republicans for a simple reason: both start with R.
[[PAUSE 0.5]]
Within a few years, "red state" and "blue state" were everyday words.
Neither party ever made these colors official. They came from TV maps, and weeks of coverage likely made them stick.

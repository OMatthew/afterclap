Why are Republican states red and Democratic states blue? Neither party ever made those colors official. In 1976, NBC built a giant lit-up map of the states for election night and colored Republicans blue and Democrats red. Roy Wetzel, who ran its election unit, said they borrowed the idea from Britain, where the Conservatives are blue. Other networks used their own schemes, so for about two decades a state's color could depend on the channel and the year. ABC and CBS came to use red for Republicans in the 1980s, CNN in 1992, and NBC by the mid-1990s.

Then came 2000. Bush against Gore went undecided for five weeks of recounts and court cases, from Election Day on Nov. 7 to the Supreme Court's decision on Dec. 12, and the same red-and-blue map stayed on TV night after night. Two days after the vote, the New York Times ran a color county map with Bush in red. Archie Tse, then a senior graphics editor there, later said: "I just decided 'red' begins with 'r,' 'Republican' begins with 'r.'" Within a few years, "red state" and "blue state" were everyday words; the American Dialect Society named "red state, blue state, purple state" its word of the year for 2004. Coverage of the long count is the likely reason the scheme stuck.

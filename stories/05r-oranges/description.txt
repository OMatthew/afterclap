Why did Florida dye its oranges orange? Because a ripe orange can still be green: cool nights break down the green in the peel, and where nights stay warm, it stays. From the 1930s, Florida growers dyed some of them, in a fight that went all the way to the Supreme Court.

The first dye, FD&C Red No. 32, was struck off in 1955. The Court said in 1958 that a harmful color couldn't be allowed even in small amounts. Congress then let in Citrus Red No. 2 "pending" a new color law; that law, the Color Additive Amendments of 1960, is the one the FDA is now using to remove Red No. 3 (the old maraschino-cherry dye) from food. Citrus Red No. 2 was listed permanently in 1963; no batch has been certified since 2020, and in July 2026 the FDA proposed revoking it as abandoned.

Florida once dyed its oranges orange.
[[PAUSE 0.3]]
Why would an orange need dye? Because a ripe orange can be green. Cool nights break down the green in the peel. Where nights stay warm, it stays.
So in the 1930s, Florida growers started dyeing them orange.
Then that dye failed new government safety tests, and it was banned. The growers sued. It's just a trace on the peel, they said.
The Supreme Court ruled against them, unanimously. If a dye is found harmful, it can't be used at all. Not even a trace.
So Congress approved a different dye, temporarily. Growers used it for about sixty years, then quietly stopped. It was never banned. Now the FDA wants to take it off the approved list.

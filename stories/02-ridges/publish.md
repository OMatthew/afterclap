**Notes**
- "Nearly half": Haynes, a Mint official, put the loss at "at least 45 per Cent"; an Exchequer sample weighed about half what it should.
- "Ridged edges": the smaller milled coins were grained (ridged); crowns had lettered edges.
- "To help pay for that": the house duty was the headline measure for the deficiency, not the whole bill.
- Most surviving bricked-up windows date from the 1700s, when the rates were heavier (the tax ran until 1851); the script only says "a few of those blank windows are still there."
- Left out on purpose: "daylight robbery" (no evidence it comes from the window tax) and Newton inventing the milled edge (he didn't).

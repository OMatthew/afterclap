In 1699, a girl named Jane told a court about the night her mother was killed.
She said they had just woken from their "first sleep."

[[PAUSE 0.3]]

Centuries later, a historian read that, and stopped. First sleep? Then what was the second?
He found both in diaries, plays and doctors' books. About two thousand times.
Many Europeans once slept twice a night, with an hour or so awake around midnight.
In that hour, people prayed, talked, or visited the neighbours.
But as lamps got brighter, the two sleeps became one, and the phrase was forgotten.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In one experiment, volunteers kept in darkness fourteen hours a night began sleeping in two halves.
Researchers still debate how common two sleeps were.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Jane never explained what "first sleep" meant.
She didn't need to. Back then, everyone knew.

In 1699, a girl named Jane told an English court about the night her mother died.
She said the two of them had just woken from their "first sleep."

[[PAUSE 0.3]]

Centuries later, a historian read that, and stopped. First sleep? Then what was the second?
He found it everywhere: in diaries, doctors' books, and plays. About two thousand mentions.
For centuries, many Europeans slept twice a night. To bed after dark, awake for an hour around midnight, then back to sleep until dawn.
But brighter lamps and later nights closed the gap, and the phrase was forgotten.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Researchers still argue about how common it was.
But volunteers kept in the dark fourteen hours a night began to sleep in two halves.
And sleep doctors say that waking for a while in the night can be normal.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Jane never explained what "first sleep" meant.
She didn't need to. Back then, everyone knew.

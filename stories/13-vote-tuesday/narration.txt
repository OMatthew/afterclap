Why do Americans vote on a Tuesday?
The usual answer is buggies and Sundays: farmers needed a day to ride to the polls without traveling on the Sabbath.
But in the debates we could read over the 1845 law, nobody says that.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Back then, each state picked its presidential electors on its own day, during about a month before those electors met to choose the president.
And lawmakers feared people could cross state lines and vote twice.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
So Congress picked one day for every state.
One draft said "the first Tuesday in November."
But in some years, that fell on November first, just outside the allowed window.
Lawmakers explained the fix: "the Tuesday after the first Monday." In those years, that moved the date a week later, inside the window.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
And why Tuesday at all? The debates we could read don't say. One lawmaker who helped write it called the exact day "of no importance."
[[PAUSE 0.5]]
Congress later used the same rule for the House and the Senate. That's why this year's Election Day is Tuesday, November third, though most states also offer early voting.

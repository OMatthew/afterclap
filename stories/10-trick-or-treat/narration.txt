Why do we say "trick or treat"? Because it started as a threat.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

A hundred years ago, Halloween meant pranks: gates carried off, wagons hauled onto roofs.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1917, an Ontario newspaper printed the earliest known use of "tricks or treats." Gangs of kids said it at the door. Pay them off, the paper said, and your house was safe. Until another gang came along that hadn't heard about the deal.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

By the 1970s, the fear flipped. Now the danger was the treat. Parents were warned that strangers hid poison and razor blades in it.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

But a sociologist has tracked these reports all the way back to 1958. He hasn't found one child killed or badly hurt by a stranger's candy. The most famous death, in 1974, was a boy poisoned by his own father.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Still, homemade treats got thrown out. Only candy in a factory wrapper counted as safe.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

Kids once got treats to stop a real threat. Now the treats come sealed, against a killer nobody has found.

Why do we say "trick or treat"? Because it started as a threat.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

A hundred years ago, Halloween night meant pranks, like gates hauled up onto roofs.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1917, an Ontario newspaper printed the earliest known "tricks or treats." Gangs of kids called it at the door. Give them a coin, the paper said, and your house was safe, until another gang came along that hadn't heard about the deal.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Then the threat turned around. In the 1970s, parents were warned that strangers hid poison and razor blades in Halloween candy. A sociologist has checked every reported case back to 1958. He hasn't found one child killed or badly hurt by a stranger's candy. The most famous death, in 1974? A boy poisoned by his own father.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Still, homemade treats got thrown out. Only candy in a factory wrapper counted as safe.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

So the treat that once paid off the trick now comes sealed against a trick no one can show ever happened.

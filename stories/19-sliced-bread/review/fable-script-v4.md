## 2. Cold-listener clarity check (v4)

**Chain, told back after one listen.** In 1943 the U.S. made selling sliced bread illegal, and that lasted under two months. Sliced bread was new, but the country was at war and officials thought it wasteful, partly because of paper: a sliced loaf needs heavier wax-paper wrapping, and officials feared a shortage. So bakeries stopped slicing and people cut loaves by hand; one mother wrote to the Times about household "morale and saneness". After the outcry the ban was dropped; officials said it saved less than expected and there was enough wax paper anyway. The Times headline: housewives' thumbs safe again. Answer: partly to save wax paper in wartime, and it wasn't worth it.

**Hook payoff.** "Illegal to sell sliced bread": paid off by "to officials, it looked wasteful" and "Partly, it was paper...". "It lasted less than two months": paid off by "After the outcry, the ban was dropped." Both hook items are the story, not tangents.

**Pronouns.**
- **"It lasted less than two months" (fix recommended).** Meant: the ban. But no noun for the ban has been said yet: line 1 says "made it illegal to sell sliced bread", so the only nouns on the table are the government and sliced bread, plus a dummy "it". Most listeners will infer "the ban", but the very next sentence starts "Sliced bread was only fifteen years old", so two durations land back to back (two months, fifteen years) and for a beat they can sound like they are about the same thing. Say the noun: "The ban lasted less than two months." Same word count, and "the ban" is then established for the ending.
- "it looked wasteful": sliced bread, restated at the start of that line. Clear.
- "Partly, it was paper": "it" is the waste / the reason. Loose, but the sentence is four words and the next one explains it. Passes. (For a beat it can be heard as "sliced bread was partly paper"; it recovers at once.)
- **"officials feared it would run short" (blocking).** Meant: wax paper. But "wax-paper" here is only a modifier inside "wax-paper wrapping", and the subject of the sentence is "A sliced loaf". On one listen "it" can attach to the loaf (bread running short in wartime is a natural thing to hear) or to the wrapping. v3 said the noun ("wax paper might run short"); this edit swapped it for a pronoun. The clarity check says to rewrite any pronoun that could point two ways. Fix: "and officials feared wax paper would run short." One more word, and it plants "wax paper" as a noun for the payoff line.
- "it had saved less": the ban, named in the sentence before. Clear.
- "they'd expected": officials, same sentence. Clear.
- "put it in a headline": the end of the ban. Clear.

**Is the ending still clear without "Less than two months later"?** Yes. The duration is now given once, up front, and "After the outcry, the ban was dropped" needs no time stamp to make sense. Saying it twice would have been a repeated beat; removing the second one was right. The listener who forgot the number loses nothing needed for the chain.

**"The outcry" after cutting the families line (not blocking).** With "But families didn't take it quietly" gone, the only protest the listener has heard is one mother's letter, so "the outcry" asks them to take that letter as standing for many. It holds, because the quote is vivid and "sawing loaves by hand" sets up the annoyance, but it is thinner than v3. If length is wanted back, this is the place: for example "Complaints poured in. One mother wrote to The New York Times..." would need a source line for volume of complaints (TIME 1943 and Smithsonian describe the public reaction), so check the fact table before using it.

**Other one-listen notes.**
- "Needs heavier wax-paper wrapping" no longer says why. Without "or it dries out" the listener is told a fact with no reason. Not false and not blocking, but "to stay fresh" (three words) would restore the because. Optional.
- "Saneness", "only fifteen years old", "Thumbs" (set up by "sawing loaves by hand"): unchanged from v3; same notes.
- Chimes: "Partly, it was paper" then "wax-paper" and "wax paper": plant and payoff, not a chime.

**Repeated punchlines, over-explaining.** None. The duplicate "less than two months" is gone.

**Insider words, question pivots.** None.

**But / so.** Illegal (and short-lived); but war, so officials saw waste; so bakeries stopped; so people sawed by hand; (but) one mother wrote; so, after that, the ban was dropped. The explicit "but" turn was cut with the families line; the mother's letter carries it, just. No "and then" beat.

**Stakes, re-hook, head fake, ending.** The early tease now does the re-hook's job from the top (why so short?), so losing the mid-Short line is acceptable. "Enough wax paper after all" is still fair in hindsight, and will be fairer once "wax paper" is said as a noun in the reason line. The headline remains a new, true button.

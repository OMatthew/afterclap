In 1943, the U.S. government made it illegal to sell sliced bread.

[[PAUSE 0.3]]

Sliced bread was only fifteen years old. But America was at war, and to officials, it looked wasteful.
A sliced loaf needs heavier wax-paper wrapping, or it dries out. And flour had just gotten pricier, so they wanted to keep bread cheap.
So bakeries had to stop slicing.
But families didn't take it quietly.
People went back to sawing loaves by hand. One mother wrote to The New York Times that sliced bread mattered to "the morale and saneness of a household."
New York's mayor complained too.
Less than two months later, after the outcry, the ban was dropped. Officials said it hadn't saved as much as they'd hoped, and there was enough wax paper after all.
The Times put it in a headline: "Housewives' Thumbs Safe Again."

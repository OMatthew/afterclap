In 1943, the U.S. government made it illegal to sell sliced bread.
[[PAUSE 0.3]]
Sliced bread was only fifteen years old. And in the middle of World War Two, it looked wasteful. A sliced loaf needs heavier wrapping to stay fresh, and wax paper was scarce. Flour had gotten pricier, too, and officials wanted to keep bread cheap.
So bakeries had to stop slicing.
Families went back to sawing loaves at home. One woman wrote to The New York Times: without sliced bread, she had to do the slicing for toast. New York's mayor complained too.
Less than two months later, the ban was over. It hadn't saved much, and there turned out to be plenty of wax paper.
The Times ran the headline: "Housewives' Thumbs Safe Again."

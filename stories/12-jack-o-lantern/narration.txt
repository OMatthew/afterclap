Why do we carve pumpkins on Halloween? Because it started as a prank, to scare people in the dark. Halloween came later.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In the 1600s, a jack-o'-lantern was a man carrying a lantern at night, or a ghostly light over a marsh.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

But in England, a turnip cut like a skull, with a candle inside, was already a scare by 1640. In one county, boys set them on hedges along the road to frighten people walking past.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In America, boys did the same with pumpkins, and called them jack-o'-lanterns. A children's book from 1838 explains how: dig it out, cut a face, put in a candle, and carry it into the dark. But the book never mentions Halloween.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

So how did it reach Halloween? In the late 1800s, historians say, Americans turned Halloween into a night for parties, and the pumpkins came along. By 1885, a magazine said kids made them for Halloween.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

Some say it all comes from an Irish tale about a man named Jack, who tricked the devil and was left to wander with a lantern. But the oldest version we found, printed in Dublin in 1836, has the lantern, and no turnip or pumpkin. In the oldest records, the carved face is just a prank.

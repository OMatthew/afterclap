Why do we carve pumpkins on Halloween? You may have heard it comes from an Irish tale about a man named Jack. But the oldest carved lanterns we found were a prank, to scare people in the dark.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In the 1600s, a jack-o'-lantern meant a man carrying a lantern at night, or a ghostly light over a marsh. But the carved face was a different thing.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In England, a turnip cut like a skull, with a candle inside, already scared people by 1640. Around 1800, boys in one county set them on hedges to frighten people walking past.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In America, boys used pumpkins, and called them jack-o'-lanterns. A children's book from 1838 explains how: dig it out, cut a face, put in a candle, and carry it into the dark. But the book never mentions Halloween.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
So how did a prank lantern become a Halloween one? Partly, historians say, because in the late 1800s Americans turned Halloween into a party night, decorated with the harvest, pumpkins included. By 1885, a magazine said kids made them for Halloween.
[[PAUSE 0.5]]
As for Jack, who tricked the devil and was left to wander with a lantern: the oldest version we found was printed in Dublin in 1836, almost two hundred years after that turnip. It has no turnip or pumpkin. On the record, the prank came first.

Why do we carve pumpkins on Halloween?
The usual answer is an Irish tale: a man named Jack tricked the devil, and was left to wander the earth with a coal inside a carved turnip.
But the oldest carved lanterns we found were a prank, to scare people in the dark.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In England, a turnip cut like a skull, with a candle inside, already scared people by 1640.
Around 1800, boys in one county set them on hedges to frighten people walking past.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
In America, boys used pumpkins instead, and called them jack-o'-lanterns.
A children's book from 1838 shows how to make one: dig it out, cut a face, put in a candle, and carry it into the dark.
But the book never mentions Halloween.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
So how did a prank lantern become a Halloween one?
Partly, historians say, because after the Civil War, Americans made Halloween a party night.
They decorated with the harvest, including pumpkins.
[[PAUSE 0.5]]
As for Jack's tale, the oldest version we found was printed in Dublin in 1836.
It has a lantern, but no coal, no turnip, no pumpkin.
The story explains a ghostly light that floats over marshes at night.
People called that light a jack-o'-lantern long before the name was given to a carved face.

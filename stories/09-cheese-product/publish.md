**Description**

Why do some cheese slices say "cheese product"? Because of one ingredient. US law sets a recipe for "pasteurized process cheese food" (21 CFR 133.173): cheese plus a list of optional dairy ingredients such as cream, milk, skim milk, buttermilk and whey. Kraft Singles were sold as cheese food, but Kraft was also using milk protein concentrate, a powder made mostly by ultrafiltering milk down to its protein. In 2002 most US supply was imported, mainly from the EU and New Zealand, and dairy groups pressed the FDA to act. That summer FDA inspectors found milk protein concentrate on the labels at Kraft plants; it isn't on the cheese-food list. In November 2002 Kraft announced it would rename Singles "pasteurized prepared cheese product," a name with no standard of identity, so it could keep the ingredient, and the FDA's warning letter of December 18, 2002 said the "cheese food" labels were misbranded. Today's Kraft Singles American slices are labeled "Pasteurized Prepared Cheese Product" and still list milk protein concentrate.

## Optional (lead's call, none required)

1. **"This time, they're holding on."** Clear enough to pass. "They" has one candidate (Macy's, which the line before treats as singular with "its"; no other plural is near), and "holding on" answers "letting the giant balloons go" from line 2. The only soft spot is that "holding on" with no object can be heard for half a second as "hanging in there". If the cut's last drawing shows handlers on the ropes, leave it. If the lead wants it airtight by ear alone:
   > This time, they're holding on to it.
2. **"a department store" to "Macy's".** Works without the spoken question: Macy's is a household name in the US, "Thanksgiving parade" confirms it, and the on-screen question names Macy's during line 1. A listener who doesn't know Macy's would still follow the story (a store, a parade, a prize). No change needed. Do not add "the store, Macy's," to line 2; it slows the line that carries the stakes.
3. **Wednesday morning re-check (time-sensitive lines).** "is flying" depends on Thursday. The only forecast I found (Fox 5 NY, Nov 19) gives no wind numbers and says the early-week storm should clear by Thursday, with a chance of breezy conditions if it slows. NYC grounds giant balloons at 23 mph sustained or 34 mph gusts. Fallback line if the forecast turns: "Macy's is bringing a blue hippo back." Also re-check that the Blue Hippo is still in the parade (Fiona news).

## Does the opening work by ear without the spoken question?

Yes, and better than v1.
- **Why the balloons matter:** line 1 leaves one puzzle (why would a store want ships to look for a hippo in the sky?). Line 2 answers half of it at once: "giant balloons" let go, with a prize for bringing one back. The listener can already guess the hippo is one of them; "the tiger balloon" then "But the blue hippo floated past..." confirm it. Nothing is left hanging.
- **"Back then, Macy's..."**: "Back then" hooks onto "In 1931", and "Macy's" lands as the department store just mentioned. No second store or actor is in play, so it can't point anywhere else.
- **Stakes:** the prize arrives at about 12-13 s, a few seconds earlier than in v1 because the question is gone.
- The hook-test rule ("the question follows in the first few seconds") is met by the on-screen question; the panel's exit point was the spoken restatement, so cutting it is the right call.

## Cold listen (clarity check 1-5)

1. **Chain back:** In 1931 a store asked ships to look for a blue hippo in the sky. Macy's used to end its parade by releasing the giant balloons, with a prize for returning one, so people chased them: a ferry crew pulled the tiger balloon from the East River, and the cat burned on a New Jersey power line. The hippo drifted past the Empire State Building and wasn't caught, so Macy's radioed the liners and quadrupled the reward. People called to say they had it; nobody brought it back. This year a blue hippo flies again, and this time it is held. The on-screen question is answered at "So Macy's radioed the ocean liners".
2. **Hook pay-offs:** "department store" in "Back then, Macy's..."; "ships at sea" in "So Macy's radioed the ocean liners"; "blue hippopotamus" in "But the blue hippo floated past...". All part of the answer.
3. **Pronouns:** "chased them" = the balloons. "saying they had found" = the callers. "brought it back" = the hippo (named in the sentence before). "its hundredth parade" = Macy's. "they're holding on" = Macy's and its handlers; see Optional 1.
4. **Heard wrong:** nothing. "The cat" now follows "the tiger balloon", so it is heard as a balloon.
5. **Repeats / over-explaining:** none. No question pivot. The ending does not restate the hook, and no longer echoes Short 18's "they stay roped down".

Pre-send check: no insider words ("ocean liners" is plain); "kept going" is indirect for a reason (we don't know where it went); the hook's hippo is the topic; the thing the story turns on (the reward) is named plainly; every fact does work.
Story engine: stakes by about 12 s; every beat joins with "so" or "but"; re-hook at "But the blue hippo ... kept going"; line lengths vary, with short lines landing the turns.
Numbers: three (1931, four times, hundredth).
Length: 126 words, inside the 110-130 target.

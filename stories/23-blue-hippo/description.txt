Why did Macy's ask ships to look for a blue hippo? From 1928 to 1932, Macy's ended its Thanksgiving parade by letting the giant balloons go, and offered a reward to whoever brought one back. After the 1931 parade, the crew of a Greenpoint ferry fished the tiger balloon out of the East River, and the cat drifted to New Jersey, floated into a high-tension wire and burned.

The Blue Hippo floated past the Empire State Building, then only months old, and kept going. On Dec. 2, 1931, The New York Times reported that Macy's had sent radiograms to the captains of the transatlantic liners at sea, asking them to watch for it, and had multiplied the reward by four. People phoned in claiming they had found it, but it was never recovered; it was thought to be somewhere over the ocean. In Macy's own words, every balloon from that parade was accounted for except the Blue Hippopotamus. By 1933, Macy's had stopped releasing balloons.

Macy's announced the Blue Hippo's return in August. After Fiona, the Cincinnati Zoo's hippo, died on November 19, Macy's said the balloon's handlers will wear a purple patch in her memory.

Why is Thanksgiving on the fourth Thursday of November?
It used to be the last Thursday. Abraham Lincoln picked it, and later presidents usually kept it.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Then came 1939. That November had five Thursdays, so Thanksgiving would land on the very last day of the month.
Store owners were worried. Back then, most people waited until after Thanksgiving to start their Christmas shopping. A late Thanksgiving meant a short shopping season, in the middle of the Great Depression.
So they asked President Franklin Roosevelt to move Thanksgiving up a week. He did.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
The country split. Some states followed him. Others kept the old date. Texas took both days off.
Critics called Roosevelt's early holiday "Franksgiving." Families in neighboring states sat down to turkey a week apart.
[[PAUSE 0.4]]
Did the extra week help the stores? Roosevelt later admitted it didn't work. Most stores said it made little difference.
In 1941, Congress made it law: Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday of November.
Most years, the fourth Thursday is also the last. But some Novembers have five.

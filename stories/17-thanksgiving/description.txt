Why is Thanksgiving on the fourth Thursday of November? For decades it was the last Thursday, the date Abraham Lincoln set in 1863. Then came 1939, when November had five Thursdays and Thanksgiving would have fallen on Nov. 30. Back then, most people waited until after Thanksgiving to begin their Christmas shopping, so business owners, worried about a short shopping season during the Depression, lobbied President Franklin Roosevelt to move the holiday up a week. He moved it to Nov. 23.

The country split. Some states followed the new date, others kept the old one, and Texas, Colorado and Mississippi agreed to observe both. Critics called it "Franksgiving." Families in neighboring states, such as New York and Connecticut, sat down to turkey a week apart. In May 1941, Roosevelt admitted the experiment "did not work": most stores said it hadn't made much difference in their sales. That year Congress made the date law, the fourth Thursday of November, signed on Dec. 26, 1941. Most years the fourth Thursday is also the last, but in years like 1939 it isn't.

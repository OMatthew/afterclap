**Description**

Why was margarine once dyed pink? So nobody would buy it. New Hampshire required it in 1885, and the Supreme Court struck that law down in 1898 (a color nobody will buy "amounts in law to a prohibition"). Congress then taxed yellow margarine at 10 cents a pound and white at a quarter cent (1902), so for decades margarine was sold white with a capsule of yellow dye to mix in at home.

The twist: margarine was dyed yellow from its first US patent (1873), copying the color "employed for the ordinary butter." In 1904 the Supreme Court upheld the yellow tax on margarine whose only yellow came from the dyed butter in its recipe. The 1950 law that ended the tax still requires restaurants to label yellow margarine or serve it "triangular in shape" (21 U.S.C. 347(c)). And the 1938 food law still exempts butter, cheese and ice cream from declaring artificial color (21 U.S.C. 343(k)); FDA rules do require a few dyes, such as Yellow 5 and carmine, to be named on any food, but the colors butter uses, annatto and beta-carotene, fall under the exemption (many brands list them anyway).

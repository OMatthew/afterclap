Why was margarine once dyed pink? So nobody would buy it. It was a fight over yellow, and butter's yellow often came from a dye too.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Margarine comes out pale, nearly white. Its inventor's 1873 patent says to add the yellow dye used for butter. In winter, cows eat hay instead of fresh grass, and their butter comes out paler. So dairies dyed it the yellow of summer butter.

Butter makers called yellow margarine a fraud anyway. In 1885, New Hampshire ordered margarine dyed pink. When one town's stores got pink margarine, nobody would buy it.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1898, the Supreme Court struck down New Hampshire's law. The Court said that forcing a color nobody will buy is really a ban.

So Congress taxed the yellow instead. From 1902, yellow margarine paid forty times the tax of white. For decades, much of it came white, with a bead of yellow dye to knead in at home.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1904, the Supreme Court said one dealer owed the yellow tax, though he'd added no dye. The yellow came from the butter in his recipe. And that butter was dyed.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

The tax ended in 1950. One rule from that year is still federal law. Restaurants serving yellow margarine without a label must cut it into triangles.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

And butter? Since 1938, federal law has let butter skip saying it's dyed. Cheese and ice cream too.

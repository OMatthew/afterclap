# Afterclap Short 06: "Why was margarine once dyed pink?"

**Status:** Narration v3.1 (226 words, about 82 s). Reviews done (Fable cold listens, Fable fact checks, three personas, Astra). Voice v2 (81.2 s), Matthew: ready. Cut v2 rendering (after Fable's visual review). Target Mon Oct 12, noon CT, if Matthew OKs the voice and cut in time.
**Promise kept from Short 05:** "And why was margarine once dyed pink? That's another story." The hook asks exactly that and answers "so nobody would buy it" in the second line.
**Groove check:** the usual telling (pink laws, the Supreme Court, the yellow dye bead, Wisconsin) is 99% Invisible / Smithsonian / National Geographic. Our edge is the far end: margarine was dyed yellow from its first patent to copy butter, which was dyed first; in 1904 a dealer owed the yellow tax because of the dyed butter in his recipe; and federal law since 1938 lets butter (and cheese and ice cream) skip saying it's dyed. Skipped as groove: Napoleon III's prize, Wisconsin's taste test, trans fats.
**Next tease (to verify before voicing):** "And why does Swiss cheese have fewer holes now?" Bridge: hay (winter hay makes pale butter; specks of hay in milk make Swiss cheese holes) and cheese in the last scene (the 1938 exemption names cheese). Backup: see research/THREAD.md.

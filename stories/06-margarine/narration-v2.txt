Why was margarine once dyed pink? So nobody would buy it. The fight was over yellow, and butter's yellow often came from dye too.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Margarine comes out nearly white. Its inventor's 1873 patent says to add the yellow dye used for butter. Butter used it first. In winter, cows eat hay instead of grass, and butter comes out paler. So dairies dyed it yellow.

Butter makers still called yellow margarine a fake. In 1885, New Hampshire passed a law that margarine had to be pink. When stores in one city got pink margarine, nobody would buy it.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1898, the Supreme Court threw that law out. The Court said forcing a color that won't sell is really a ban.

So in 1902, Congress taxed yellow margarine instead. Its tax was forty times the tax on white. For decades, makers sold it white, with a capsule of yellow dye that families mixed in at home.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

The tax ended in 1950. But one rule from that year is still US law. A restaurant serving yellow margarine has to label each piece, or cut it into a triangle.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

And butter? A 1938 US law still says butter doesn't have to tell you it's dyed. Neither do cheese and ice cream.

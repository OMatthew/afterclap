Why was margarine once dyed pink? So nobody would buy it. The fight was over yellow, and butter's yellow often came from dye too.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Margarine comes out nearly white. Its inventor's 1873 patent says to add the yellow dye that butter already used. In winter, cows ate hay instead of grass, and butter came out paler. So dairies dyed it yellow.

Butter makers still called yellow margarine a fake. In 1885, New Hampshire passed a law that margarine had to be pink. A few states copied it. When stores in one of those states got pink margarine, nobody would buy it.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1898, the Supreme Court ruled against New Hampshire's law. The Court said forcing a color that won't sell is really a ban.

So in 1902, Congress taxed yellow margarine instead, at forty times the rate on white. To avoid that tax, makers sold margarine white for decades, with a capsule of yellow dye that families mixed in at home.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

The tax ended in 1950. But one rule from that year is still U.S. law. A restaurant serving yellow margarine has to label each piece, or cut it into a triangle.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

As for butter, under a 1938 U.S. law, it can be dyed without saying so. The same goes for cheese and ice cream.

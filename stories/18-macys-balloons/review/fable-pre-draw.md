# Short 18 (Macy's balloons): independent review before drawing, 2026-11-10

Basis: narration v2, words.json timings (47.9 s, 136 words), PROMPT_art.md, the research file, CHANNEL_RULES "Hook and packaging", Shorts 11 and 17 scripts, the Nov 3 analytics. I did not hear the audio. Facts spot-checked against TIME (Dec 1932) and Hatching Cat; they hold.

**Verdict: the story and shape are the best since 11. Not quite ready to draw: one voice re-render and four changes to the drawing list first (below). All are small.**

## 1. Hook

- The fact is strong and the premise passes "wait, what?". The crash also opens a loop (what happened to her?) that stays open until 39 s, which is what 17 lacked.
- Weak spot: the first two seconds of audio are "In 1932," then 0.8 s of silence, then "a student pilot". The strange part ("giant balloon cat") lands at 4.3-5.5 s. So the first 2 s depend entirely on the picture and the type.
- The on-screen question and the voice do not compete: the voice says only a date during the type's window. But nine words in 1.5 s is too much to read. Hold the type to about 2.5-3 s, or shorten it on screen ("Why did Macy's let its balloons fly away?").
- Better opening line (clearly better, since a re-render is needed anyway, see 3): **"A student pilot once flew her plane straight into a giant balloon cat."** A person in the first second, the cat about 1.5 s sooner, no dead air. Move the year to the callback: **"In 1932, a student steered right into the cat."** That also puts the two years in order (1928, then 1932) and makes the return to the opening unmistakable.

## 2. Script

- Shape works: crash (stakes), prize hunt and tug-of-war (people in conflict), warning (turn), spin and rescue (climax), releases end (payoff). The title is answered at 8-11 s ("on purpose, as a prize hunt"), and the crash carries the rest. Nothing unsupported.
- Add "after the parade" and the holiday, once: "Because after its Thanksgiving parade, Macy's let them go on purpose, as a prize hunt." Right now a cold listener never hears when they were let go, and the word Thanksgiving is never spoken.
- "pulled the plane out just above them": "pulled out" is pilot talk and "them" is far from "rooftops". Plainer: "Her instructor grabbed the controls and leveled out just above them." Optional.
- Ending: "Nobody wants another flying cat" is a quip, but it is earned, brings the cat back and is short. Keep. It only works fully if the last drawing is a cat (see 4).
- Numbers: three (1932, 1928, a hundred dollars). Fine.

## 3. Pacing (from words.json)

- **Rushed: "Why was a parade balloon up there at all?" runs 6.0-7.8 s, 9 words in 1.8 s, about 5.1 words/s against about 3.0-3.5 elsewhere.** It is the pivot of the whole Short. Re-render it (the channel rule for rushed lines).
- **No breath after the rescue:** "just above them" ends at 38.96 and "By the next parade" starts 0.36 s later, the shortest sentence gap in the Short, right at the climax. Add a [[PAUSE 0.5]] there.
- Slow: the 0.8 s gap after "In 1932" (gone with the new opening).
- The rest is even. No dragging stretch; 48 s is right.

Changes I would make (still 9 drawings):

1. **Draw the letting go.** The title's image is not in the plan. Replace 2 and 3 with one drawing: handlers opening their hands, ropes dangling, the balloon lifting off over rooftops, faces looking up. Hold drawing 1 through the 1.8 s question.
2. **Make the last balloon the same cat,** roped down by a long line of handlers. It pays off "another flying cat" and matches the first frame. It can cover 39.3-47.7 (pan along the ropes), or use the freed slot for a balloon being deflated or folded on "stopped letting the balloons go".
3. **Known-character risk is real with five animals.** A "friendly round dog" balloon will drift toward Snoopy (a Macy's staple), a bear toward Pooh, an elephant toward Dumbo, a smiling cat toward Felix (the famous Macy's cat) or Garfield, a tiger toward Tigger or Tony. Cut to two designs: a striped tabby cat (the real one was a yellow-striped tom; say "not black, no bow tie, no big white eyes") and a plain tiger. Use the cat or a simple fish or dragon for the generic balloons. Fewer designs also helps the viewer follow "the cat".
4. **Faces:** goggles hide the eyes, and the pilot is tiny beside the balloon. Ask for goggles pushed up on the forehead, eyes and open mouth visible, in 1 and 8. The cat's own face is the big face in frame one; make sure it reads.
5. **Prize drawing:** swap the hands and tag for a grinning person hauling a saggy balloon with a tag on it (paint on the balloon). People, not objects.

Paint: balloon to balloon is a natural chain and the biggest paint the channel has had since 01: cat, released balloon, tiger, the wrap on the wing, the roped cat. Two blood checks: the tiger tearing in half, and the red wrap on a plane spinning down. Use flat fills with the calm splat (no specks, drips or satellites) on both; never red on the pilots.

Reuse: stories/11-ragamuffin-day/art/big-balloon.png may save a drawing.

## 5. Title

Keep "Why did Macy's let its parade balloons fly away?" (48 characters, no answer given away, has the search words for the two weeks before Thanksgiving). It pairs well with the spoken fact: two different hooks instead of the same one twice. A pilot-led title would repeat the first line and promise a crash that did not happen.

## Must-fix, most important first

1. Re-render the voice: the rushed pivot question (5.1 words/s) and a pause after the rescue. Take the new opening line, "In 1932" on the callback, and "after its Thanksgiving parade" in the same pass.
2. Add the letting-go drawing (replacing parade-street and balloon-sky) and make the final roped balloon the cat; cover "stopped letting the balloons go", which has no drawing now.
3. Cut the balloon animals to an original striped cat and a tiger; drop the dog, bear and elephant.
4. Goggles up so the faces show in drawings 1 and 8; a person instead of hands for the prize.
5. Calm, flat paint on the torn tiger and the wing wrap.
6. Hold the on-screen question about 2.5-3 s or shorten it.

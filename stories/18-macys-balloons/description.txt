Why did Macy's let its parade balloons fly away? From 1928, when the Thanksgiving parade ended, Macy's released its giant balloons over New York and offered a prize to whoever brought one back: $100 in 1928. People fought over them. That year a tiger balloon came down on a rooftop in Richmond Hill, Queens, and a tug-of-war tore it to pieces. Pilots joined the hunt too, until Macy's said aviators who downed a balloon in flight wouldn't get a prize.

In 1932, a 22-year-old student pilot flew into the Tom-Cat balloon over Queens. Its fabric wrapped around her wing and the plane went into a spin; her instructor took over and pulled it out just above the rooftops, and they landed safely. By the next parade, Macy's had stopped releasing the balloons. Today, handlers keep them roped down the whole route.

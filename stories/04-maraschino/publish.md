**Description**

Why are maraschino cherries so red? Because the cherry isn't. The red is dye. And for nearly thirty years, the government called them imitations.

Maraschino cherries are preserved in a brine that bleaches them pale, then dyed red and soaked in almond syrup. The name comes from maraschino, a liqueur from the Dalmatian coast, now Croatia, where dark marasca cherries were kept. Prohibition gets the blame for the American fake, but the fake came first: by 1910, federal officials were fining makers and seizing "maraschino cherries" with no maraschino in them. In 1912 they had to be called imitation; in 1940 the FDA accepted what shoppers meant, a cherry dyed red. One dye found in 1910, erythrosine, is today's Red No. 3, which US food makers must stop using by January 15, 2027.

Why is a dime smaller than a nickel, when it's worth twice as much?

Blame a Civil War panic, a man with a nickel mine, and a goblin.

[[PAUSE 0.6]]

Dimes were silver, and ten cents of silver is small.

Then the Civil War hit. People hoarded gold and silver, and stores gave change in postage stamps.

[[PAUSE 0.6]]

Congress wanted coins of cheap metal. And nearly all the nickel mined in America belonged to one man. Joseph Wharton.

In 1866 came the first five-cent nickel. Cheap metal, so its size was up to Congress. A committee bumped up the weight, officially to make an even five grams. Conveniently, that took more nickel.

The silver left the dime in 1965. The size stayed.

[[PAUSE 0.6]]

Wharton made millions on nickel, and in 1881 he founded a business school. The Wharton School.

[[PAUSE 0.7]]

And the goblin? German miners kept digging up ore that looked like copper, but gave none. So they blamed a goblin, and named the ore after him.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Kupfernickel. Copper goblin.

[[PAUSE 0.6]]

When a chemist found a new metal inside it, he kept the goblin's name.

[[PAUSE 0.9]]

And why does the dime have ridges, but not the nickel? That's another story.

## Narration v1 (narration.txt)

```text
Why does a slice of American cheese melt so smoothly? A special salt keeps the fat mixed in. An early patent even called the salt a laxative.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Cheese holds fat in a network of protein, linked partly by calcium. Heat the cheese too much, and fat can leak out as grease.

American cheese is made by melting cheese with salts such as sodium phosphate. The salts grab some of that calcium. The loosened proteins take up water and hold the fat in tiny droplets, so the melt stays smooth when you heat the slice again.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Cheesemakers putting cheese in cans needed that smooth melt too. In 1921, a patent for the Phenix Cheese Company used sodium phosphate to keep fat from floating to the top. The cheese stayed mixed when the sealed cans were heated again to kill germs.

But the patent also praised the salt as "a mild laxative." The inventor admitted he wasn't sure how the salt worked in cheese.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

And why do some slices say "cheese product" on the wrapper? That's another story.
```

## Story choice

The answer arrives in the second sentence; the patent surprise arrives within the first 27 words, roughly ten seconds. The causal thread is fat escaping under heat → added salts change how the proteins hold fat → the same problem during canning → the patent that credits a melting salt with a second, medicinal benefit.

The Swiss export history, Kraft’s separate sterilizing patent, Army purchases, merger, and federal recipe details would each start another explanation. They are outside this short draft. The FDA label story is reserved for the last line.

**Verified core of that next story:** Kraft’s milk protein concentrate did not fit the permitted dairy-ingredient list for pasteurized process cheese food. The FDA documented that conflict in 2002; Kraft used the cheese-product label while retaining the ingredient. The FDA letter and CFR establish the ingredient conflict; USDA’s 2004 report independently records the relabeling. The current manufacturer page confirms the label and ingredient remain in the American Singles example.

## Uncertainties and exclusions

- The precise modern sodium phosphate blend need not match Garstin’s 1921 formulation. The narration connects a shared function, not an unchanged recipe or a proven path of invention.
- The patent establishes what its author claimed. No claim is made that the cheese works as a laxative, or that the inventor discovered the modern calcium explanation.
- No universal promise that American cheese cannot separate under any conditions. Water, acidity, formulation and processing also affect texture.
- The FDA story concerns conformity to a named food standard, not whether the slice is “fake,” plastic, or below an assumed cheese percentage.

**Description**

Why does a slice of American cheese melt so smoothly? Because it has already been melted once, with a melting salt. Heat most cheese and the fat separates out as grease; melting salts (sodium citrates and phosphates) pull calcium out of the cheese's protein network, so the protein holds on to the fat and the cheese stays smooth when it melts again. US rules for pasteurized process cheese list 13 of these salts.

The idea is Swiss. Around 1911, at the cheese exporter Gerber & Co. in Thun, Walter Gerber and Fritz Stettler melted Emmentaler with melting salts and sealed it in tins, so it could be shipped overseas without spoiling. Melting salts reached American patents in 1921: George Garstin's patent for the Phenix Cheese Company used sodium phosphate to keep canned cheese from separating, and added that the salt was "a mild laxative and a liver tonic." (Kraft's better-known 1916 patent used no salt at all.)

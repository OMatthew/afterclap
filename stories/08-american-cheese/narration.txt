Why does a slice of American cheese melt so smoothly? Because it's already been melted once, with a pinch of special salt. And that trick was invented to save Swiss cheese.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Around 1911, a Swiss company was shipping Emmentaler overseas. On the long trip, the cheese could spoil. So they melted it with a citrate salt and sealed it in tins. Now it kept.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Here's how the trick works. Heat most cheese, and its protein tightens up and squeezes out the fat as grease. Calcium is what holds that protein together. The salt pulls the calcium away, so the protein relaxes and holds on to the fat. Melt it again, and it stays smooth.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

American cheesemakers soon used salts like this too. A 1921 patent said sodium phosphate kept canned cheese from separating. It also said the salt was a mild laxative. And the inventor admitted he wasn't sure how it worked.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

So the trick behind America's smooth slice was first used to get Swiss cheese across the ocean.

Why does a slice of American cheese melt so smoothly? The trick behind it was invented in Switzerland, more than a hundred years ago.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Around 1911, a cheese company in Switzerland was shipping Swiss cheese overseas. On the long trip, the cheese could spoil. So they melted it down with a pinch of special salt and sealed it in tins. Now it could survive the trip.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

Heat most cheese, and the fat leaks out as grease. That salt keeps the fat mixed in. So when you melt the cheese again, it stays smooth. And that's what every slice of American cheese is: cheese that's already been melted once, with a salt like that.

[[PAUSE 0.4]]

In 1921, an American patent used one of these salts, sodium phosphate, to keep canned cheese from separating. The patent also praised the salt as a mild laxative. And its inventor admitted he wasn't sure how it worked.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

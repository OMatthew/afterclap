# Chain channel: research check + 30-day plan

Drafted 2026-10-05. Inputs: the general YouTube research from the Bearing Fruit agent, the Early Exit video ("50,000 Subscribers in 50 Videos"), and YouTube's own docs (checked today).

**Assumptions (correct me):** new channel, zero stats. Topic = true chains of cause and effect, small start to big result, ending in the viewer's present. Audience = curious 16–35-year-olds who never saw *Connections*. Format = vertical Shorts first, 60–75 s, AI voice (Darren, no pitch change), stylized AI visuals. Matthew's time ≈ approvals only.

---

## 1. Which findings apply here

### Applies fully
- **Recommendations pull toward satisfied viewers; Shorts get a small seed audience; one weak upload doesn't hurt.** This is exactly how a new Shorts channel grows. Never delete and repost a Short.
- **Evergreen topics.** History and science chains don't go stale, so every Short can keep earning views. This is the niche's biggest structural advantage.
- **Packaging rules:** titles ≤70 chars, no episode numbers or series names, 10+ title ideas → keep 3, title and cover don't repeat each other. Add one niche rule: **never put the reveal word in the title or cover.**
- **Opening rules:** no logo, no music lead-in, no "in this video." On Shorts the window is about 1–3 seconds, not 30.
- **Outlier log:** very useful. Track 3x outliers in this niche *and* adjacent ones (science Shorts, "how X changed the world," history explainers, weird-history).
- **Cadence beats volume, and gaps hurt.** Early Exit's two-month gap cost him reach he never fully regained (his story, not data, but YouTube lists "uploading less than usual" as a cause of drops).
- **Plan the first 10 videos as one coherent set** (Early Exit). It tells YouTube who the channel is for. Same format every time, varied topics.
- **Copy with taste** (Early Exit): take the *kind* of story Connections and Paul Harvey told, never their scripts or titles.

### Policy: matters more for this niche than most
- **Inauthentic content (Partner Program):** AI-made, templated, mass-produced videos without original insight can't be monetized. AI history and Bible-story channels were among the biggest 2026 terminations. Our defense: original research, a verified fact table per video, a distinct storytelling voice, and varied visuals. Never daily templated output.
- **Since Oct 1, 2026, originality affects Shorts reach** (confirmed, Rene Ritchie). It targets re-uploaded or aggregated clips. Narration that only "describes what's on screen" doesn't count as original. Our original storytelling is fine.
- **AI disclosure:** a stylized look (painted, diorama, illustrated) needs no disclosure. Photoreal depictions of real people or real events do need the label. The label doesn't reduce reach. **Rule: keep visuals stylized; never show a photoreal face of a real person.**
- **Not Made for Kids.**

### Applies later (not in the first 30 days)
- Related Video link from Shorts to long-form (YouTube's July 2026 guide), and the "Shorts subscribers who never watch long-form" risk. Revisit when long-form starts.
- Test & Compare, CTR bands, chapters, "key moments": long-form only.
- Hype: needs the Partner Program.
- Translated titles and dubbed audio: **a real lever for an AI-narrated channel** (one ElevenLabs pass per language). Hold it until the format settles.

### Doesn't apply
- Faces in thumbnails (we're faceless, and Shorts in the feed aren't chosen by thumbnail).
- Fixed time-of-day publishing matters little for Shorts. A fixed rhythm (same days each week) still helps.

## 2. What's missing for this niche
1. **Accuracy is the brand.** History and science commenters fact-check hard. One wrong claim can define a small channel. Every Short gets a fact table before production, and a sources line in its description.
2. **Hedge causality.** Real outcomes have several causes. Say "part of the reason" or "lined up with," never "all because." (Pilot 01 v1 got this wrong; v2 fixed it.)
3. **Rights.** Use no archival photos unless they're public domain (US government works, pre-1929). Use licensed or YouTube Audio Library music to avoid Content ID claims.
4. **Shorts mechanics:** burned-in captions, frame 0 is a strong image plus 2–4 words (it doubles as the cover), narration starts at 0.0 s, and the ending can loop back to the opening line.
5. **Series without numbers.** End each Short by teasing the next story ("But that's another story."), then publish that one within a week. The "chain" idea is shown in the visuals (a thread linking each step), not said in the narration.
6. **Shares are the signal to watch.** People share "did you know" chains. Shares per 1,000 views says more than likes for this format.
7. **Trust filter on advice:** the Early Exit creator sells audits and a paid community. Keep his tactics that YouTube's docs back (consistency, coherent first 10, copying with taste) and treat his specific CTR/AVD numbers as one channel's experience.

## 3. 30-day plan

### Days 1–5: set up (Claude and agents; Matthew approves)
- [ ] Pick a visual style: 3 style frames for Pilot 01 (e.g., painted diorama, engraved illustration, paper cut-out) with a "red thread" linking each beat. Matthew picks one.
- [ ] Video pipeline: narration → visuals → captions → assembly, so a Short needs about 5 minutes of Matthew's time (approve script, approve final).
- [ ] Idea bank of 30 verified chains, tagged by pillar (science and tech / nature / history / "close calls"), direction (good / bad), and the reveal object in the viewer's life.
- [ ] Choose and plan the first 10. All the same format, with mixed pillars.
- [ ] Channel name, avatar and banner (options for Matthew).

### Days 6–30: publish 3 Shorts a week (same 3 days each week, about 12 total)
- About 10 core Shorts and 2 experiments, e.g., a 55-second cut versus 75 seconds, or a "ways the world almost ended" chain.
- Each Short: fact table → Fable or second-model review → voice → visuals → Matthew approves → publish.
- Pin a comment with sources plus "Which chain should we trace next?" (this doubles as an idea source).

### Packaging rules
- Title: a curiosity gap or question, ≤70 chars, no numbers, no series name, no reveal word.
- Cover/frame 0: 2–4 large words, a brand mark in the same spot every time, text clear of the bottom UI.

### Opening rules
- The first sentence names a concrete object or claim (≤3 s). The promise lands by 5 s. The date comes after the hook, not before.

### Metrics (weekly scorecard)
| Metric | Where | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| Stayed to watch vs. swiped away | Shorts analytics | The hook's grade. Rough rule (judgment): below ~60% means fix the first 2 seconds |
| Average % viewed | per Short | The middle's grade. Low means cut slack or shorten |
| Engaged views | per Short | Real reach (replays inflate plain views) |
| Shares per 1K views | per Short | Whether the chain was worth passing on |
| Subscribers per 1K views | per Short | Whether people want the series |
| Traffic source | per Short | Shorts feed vs. search vs. browse |

### What to change first (in this order)
1. **Swipe-away is high** → change the first line and frame 0 only.
2. **Hooks hold but % viewed is low** → cut the middle; try 55–60 s.
3. **Both fine but views stall at the seed audience** → choose bigger reveal objects (things everyone touches daily).
4. **Views but no subscribers** → make the series identity stronger (the sign-off, and a tease that pays off next week).

### Day-30 gate
Keep going if any Short breaks well past its seed audience, or if stayed-to-watch and % viewed are trending up. Otherwise change one variable for another 30 days. YouTube staff suggest about 90 days before judging a trend, so the 30-day read is directional.

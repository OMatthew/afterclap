# Afterclap: independent review (2026-11-10)

Basis: the repo docs, the Nov 3 analytics snapshot and the Oct 11 weekly, the scripts, contact sheets of frames from Shorts 01, 06, 10, 11, 14, 16 and 17, and seven covers. I did not listen to the audio. One web search for Shorts benchmarks returned only marketing blogs, so I cite no outside numbers.

## 1. Where it stands

Afterclap is a well-made, honest channel that the Shorts feed is not yet picking up. Every Short that gets seeded tops out between 900 and 2,600 views, and the channel has 53 subscribers after five weeks, so the 90-day bar (one Short past 100K, or 1,000 subscribers) is out of reach on the current path. The craft is not the problem: the look is distinctive, the facts are solid and the scripts are clear. The biggest constraint is shots on goal. Each Short costs a 30-45 KB research file, several script versions and four to six cuts, output has dropped to about one a week (16 on Nov 1, 17 on Nov 9), and two researched Shorts (13 Tuesday voting, 15 party animals) never shipped; 13 missed Election Day. The second constraint is the first two seconds, which are too quiet for a feed.

## 2. What's working, what's holding it back

**Working (keep):**
- **The paper, ink and paint look.** It is unlike anything else in the feed and cheap to render.
- **Accuracy and sources.** No change needed.
- **Short 11 (Ragamuffin Day) is the model.** It has 74% stayed-to-watch, 161 likes (about 6.6% of views, against 2-4% elsewhere) and 19 of the channel's 53 subscribers. It differs from the rest in three ways: the question is strange in itself, the frames are full of people (kids, a policeman, a scowling woman), and the story is a fight.
- **Answer-first hooks.** Short 10 ("Because it started as a threat") has 73% stayed-to-watch.
- **Dropping the tease and the film-reel ending** on 14, 16 and 17.

**Holding it back:**
- **Mild questions and slow openings.** The three weakest hooks are 14 (56%), 12 (58%) and 16 (62%). Short 14 spends its first eight seconds on "the usual story" before debunking it. Short 17 opens "It used to be the last Thursday" and holds its best material (two Thanksgivings, Texas taking both) until about 25 s.
- **Frame one is a small thin drawing on empty paper** with one line of small, light serif caption. The drawing fills roughly a third of the frame height. Nothing on screen says what the video is about until the voice does.
- **The paint has shrunk.** In 01 it splats and floods the whole dime. In 14, 16 and 17 it is a small dot. Short 16 is about red and blue states and never fills a state with colour. The channel's one signature has gone timid, partly from the blood fix.
- **Objects instead of people.** Short 17 is a calendar, a turkey, a dome and a cash register. Short 11 is characters doing things.
- **Length.** Average view duration sits at 39-50 s whatever the runtime (06 is the exception at 61 s of 85 s). Short 16 has the best % viewed mostly because it is the shortest.
- **Noise.** With 500-1,700 engaged views per Short, most differences between Shorts are within luck. Only 11's subscriber and like numbers clearly stand out.

## 4. Ten topics to make next

Chosen for a strange premise, people and timing. I have not run the competition scan on these, and several are known trivia; that is deliberate (see change 2). All need the usual two-source check.

1. **Why did Macy's once let its parade balloons fly away?** Balloons were released with reward tags; a plane hit one in 1932 and the releases stopped. Pays off 11's tease. Post by Nov 17.
2. **Why does the president pardon a turkey?** In 1947 poultry farmers angry at "poultryless Thursdays" sent crates of live chickens to the White House; the pardon itself only became official in 1989. *Verify the link between the protest and the first turkey presentation.*
3. **Why is it called Black Friday?** Philadelphia police used it for the traffic mess; shops tried "Big Friday" and failed; the "in the black" story came later. Post by Nov 20.
4. **Why does Rudolph exist?** A department store wanted a cheaper giveaway booklet in 1939, and later handed the copyright to the employee who wrote it. The red nose is made for the paint.
5. **Why was Christmas once illegal in Boston?** Puritans fined anyone caught celebrating, from 1659 to 1681.
6. **Why do we wrap presents in printed paper?** In 1917 a Kansas City shop ran out of tissue and sold envelope linings instead; the shop became Hallmark.
7. **Why are poinsettias everywhere at Christmas?** One California family kept a growing secret for decades and held most of the world market until a researcher published it. *Verify the market share and date.*
8. **Why does a ball drop on New Year's Eve?** Ports dropped balls so ships could set their clocks; New York banned the Times's fireworks in 1907, so it borrowed the idea.
9. **Why did Americans once mail their children?** Parcel post began in 1913 and stamps were cheaper than a train ticket. *Only a handful of cases, and the famous photos were staged; say so.*
10. **Why did the US ban sliced bread?** A 1943 wartime order that lasted under two months after an outcry.

## 5. Stop doing

- **Long-form and compilations for now.** The 01-09 film got about 5 views. Revisit after one Short breaks out.
- **The tease chain, the related-video relinking and the in-order playlist.** At 1-2K views they cost agent time and move nothing.
- **Polishing covers and their wording.** 90% of views come from the feed, where the cover is not shown.
- **Facebook Reels,** unless the numbers have changed: 117-214 views per reel, two page suspensions.
- **Researching Shorts that don't ship,** and tying a topic to a date without a firm publish slot.
- **Adding script rules.** The rulebook is long enough; further gains will come from topics and openings.
- **Reading small gaps in the metrics.** Decide on runs of three to five Shorts, not one.

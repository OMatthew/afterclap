# Short 17 research: "Why is Thanksgiving on the fourth Thursday of November?"

Researched 2026-11-06 (lead, web). The Claude API research run on Nov 3 failed (credits gone), so this replaces it.

| Claim | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Lincoln's Oct 3, 1863 proclamation set the last Thursday of November; presidents followed it | National Archives, Prologue blog "Thanksgiving as a Federal Holiday" (2023): https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2023/11/20/thanksgiving-as-a-federal-holiday/ ; NARA Center for Legislative Archives: https://www.archives.gov/legislative/features/thanksgiving | "regularly commemorated each year on the last Thursday of November" |
| 1939: the last Thursday fell on Nov 30, the last day of the month (Nov 1939 had five Thursdays) | NARA CLA page | |
| Business owners lobbied FDR, worried a short Christmas shopping season would hurt the recovery | NARA Prologue: "large business owners lobbied Roosevelt to move the holiday up a week"; "Concerned that the shortened Christmas shopping season might dampen the economic recovery" | Who exactly is disputed (Lew Hahn, Retail Dry Goods Association, per HistoryNet; Fred Lazarus Jr., per Wikipedia). Don't name one. |
| FDR moved it to the second-to-last Thursday (announced Aug 1939) | NARA Prologue; HistoryNet https://historynet.com/fdrs-franksgiving-debacle/ | |
| States split; some observed both days. 1939: 23 + DC followed, 22 refused; Colorado, Mississippi, Texas gave both days (Wikipedia); HistoryNet: Texas and Colorado both, Mississippi undecided; NARA: 32 vs 16 (later years) | NARA CLA; HistoryNet; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franksgiving | Say "Texas took both days off" (both sources agree on Texas). |
| "Franksgiving": nickname used by critics; coined by Atlantic City mayor Charles D. White (Vox via Wikipedia) | Wikipedia; HistoryNet subtitle | Say "critics called it", not who coined it. |
| 1941 Commerce Department survey found no significant change in retail sales | HistoryNet; Wikipedia (citing NYT, May 21, 1941) | "found no real change in sales" |
| House passed H.J. Res. 41 (last Thursday) Oct 6, 1941; Senate amended to fourth Thursday; FDR signed Dec 26, 1941; effective 1942 | NARA CLA; NARA Prologue | |
| Fourth = last Thursday in most years (5 of 7 possible calendars) | arithmetic: Nov has five Thursdays only when Nov 1 is a Wednesday or Thursday | "Most years" is safe. |

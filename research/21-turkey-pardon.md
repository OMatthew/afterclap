# Short 21 research: "Why does the president pardon a turkey?"

Researched 2026-11-20 (research agent, web). To publish Tue 2026-11-24; Thanksgiving is Thu 2026-11-26. **The 2026 ceremony is the same day as the upload: Tue Nov 24, 2:00 PM ET, South Lawn** (see Time-sensitive).

Premise test: the first "pardon" was a president's joke while reporters asked whether he'd pardon Iran-Contra figures; the bird has committed no crime; and the pardoned bird is bred for meat, so it often doesn't live long anyway (pass: "wait, what?" on all three).

Present-day angle (why it still happens): nobody ordered it. It is a trade-group photo opportunity (the National Turkey Federation has brought a bird since 1947) that picked up a joke, and it survives because both sides get something: the industry gets its turkey beside the president two days before the biggest turkey meal of the year, and the president gets a light moment with one of the office's most serious powers. In 2025 the jokes were about real pardons (autopen); real pardons are in the news, which is why the word matters now. This is interpretation: keep it hedged ("part of the reason") or leave it to the description.

## Myths

- **"Truman started the pardon."** No. The Truman Library has no record of any pardon, and he said the birds were for the table. He was only the first to receive the NTF's bird (Dec 1947).
- **"Lincoln pardoned the first turkey."** A story, not a Thanksgiving pardon. The source is reporter Noah Brooks's 1865 dispatch: a live turkey "had been brought home for the Christmas dinner, but [Tad] interceded in behalf of its life." It was a Christmas bird (said to be 1863, named Jack), written up after the fact; WHHA calls the clemency story "likely apocryphal". The reprieve "written on a card" is later embroidery. Skip it or call it a story.
- **"Kennedy pardoned a turkey."** He spared one and never said "pardon"; newspapers supplied the word. His exact words are disputed (see table).
- **"Reagan pardoned a turkey in 1987."** He joked that he *would have*; the bird was already going to a pet farm.
- **"The pardon saves the bird for a long life."** Often only a year or two; some under a year. But not always (Courage, six years).
- **"It's a real pardon."** No legal effect.
- **Unverified leads, not for the script:** that the 1947 presentation grew out of turkey growers protesting the government's "poultryless Thursdays" (seen only via Wikipedia); that animal-rights protesters were picketing nearby in 1989 (attributed to WHHA, whose main article I could not open: the URL redirects to Issuu).

# 20: First sleep (lead notes, 2026-11-20)

Lead from Matthew: X post by @SamaHoole (https://x.com/SamaHoole/status/2123615538991571054). A lead, not a source. Verify each claim; it overstates.

Claims to check (two sources each; Ekirch's own work first):
- Pre-industrial Europeans commonly slept in two segments ("first sleep", "second sleep") with a waking hour between. Source: A. Roger Ekirch, "Sleep We Have Lost" (American Historical Review, 2001) and At Day's Close (2005).
- "Nobody slept through the night" is too absolute. Yetish et al. 2015 (Current Biology) found that three present-day pre-industrial societies (Hadza, San, Tsimane) mostly did not sleep in two segments. Say "in pre-industrial Europe, it was normal", and name the debate briefly.
- The 1697 court record with a nine-year-old girl: verify it exists and is how Ekirch first found it (his account of the discovery).
- "More than 2,000 references in a dozen languages, back to ancient Greece": check Ekirch's own counts.
- Doctors' advice on which side to lie on for each half: verify.
- Faded with artificial light, late hours and industrial work; "by about the 1920s" the term had gone: Ekirch's dating.
- Thomas Wehr (NIMH), 1992: volunteers given 14 hours of darkness a night for about a month began sleeping in two bouts with a quiet waking between. Check the paper (J Sleep Res 1992) for exact setup.
Present-day angle (why it matters now): waking at 3am is often treated as insomnia; sleep researchers on whether "middle-of-the-night waking" is normal; screens and light. Don't give medical advice; one careful line at most.

## What they do
- **One persistent canvas.** A single map stays on screen; the camera pans and zooms, and each new fact is *added* to it (a colored territory, a ship, a river lighting up, a "1802" title) instead of cutting to a new picture. Lots of motion, few context switches.
- **Motion from the first frame.** Chile opens mid-zoom on a globe; by 1.6 s there's a visual gag (a chili pepper laid over Chile: "no, not that chili").
- **Scale comparisons:** Chile laid over Europe, "London to Baghdad".
- **A light touch of humor and recurring cute characters** (chibi diplomats, a face on a country).
- **Small captions** (1–3 words, low on screen): the pictures carry it.
- **Titles:** the question plus a keyword phrase and emojis ("How America Bought Louisiana 🇺🇸🤯 Napoleon's $15M Deal 🇫🇷").
- **Descriptions:** the whole story retold in a keyword-rich paragraph, then about 20 hashtags. No sources.
- **Cadence:** about three a week, the same format every time.

## What Afterclap could take (and what not)
- **Fewer, longer scenes that build.** Keep a drawing on screen across two or three beats and add to it (a second small drawing, the paint hopping within it) instead of scrolling to a new drawing for every sentence. Short 03 has 14 scenes in 79 s (one every 5.6 s); aim for about 8–10 in Short 04. This is the answer to Matthew's pace-versus-context-switching worry.
- **Motion in the first second.** Start with the paint already falling, so frame one isn't still.
- **One scale or comparison beat per Short** where the story allows it.
- **Descriptions:** add two or three plain sentences of the story (searchable words) above the sources, plus three to five hashtags. Keep the sources and the AI note; they set us apart.
- **Titles:** test a second, searchable phrase after the question. Emojis: at most one, and only as a test (groove check: everyone does it).
- **Not for us:** photoreal AI inserts, clip-art characters and generic maps. They'd break the hand-inked look.
- **Our edge they don't have:** the thread (each Short asks the next question), sources in every description, and the paint as a character.
